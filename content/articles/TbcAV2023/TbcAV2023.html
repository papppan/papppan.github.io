On the beauty ful and charming tetraquark Tbc

Dr. Padmanath Madanagopalan
14 May 2024

In his seminal work on “A schematic model of baryons and mesons” [1], Murray Gell-Mann argues that baryons (family of protons) could be composed from color ed quarks (q) in colorless forms (qqq), (qqqqq), etc., whereas mesons (family of pions) could be objects of the form (qq), (qqqq), etc., where (q) refers to the antiquarks. Despite this very farsighted prediction, most of the observed hadrons, in the past century, were successfully classified under the two simplest categories: (qq) and (qqq) generally referred to as baryons and mesons, respectively. Starting from the year 2003, there has been a bunch of discoveries at experimental facilities, like LHCb, Belle, etc., of several excited and exotic hadrons that defies this conventionally accepted picture of baryons and mesons, and calls for a description beyond these two simplest categories. Among these is the Tcc+ tetraquark, that was recently discovered by the LHCb collaboration [2] is the most recent sensation, which sparked a rapid proliferation in the scientific interest among theoreticians and experimentalists. The Tcc+ tetraquark is a hadron composed of two charm quarks, a up antiquark and a ddown antiquark. The bottom flavored cousins of Tcc+, Tbb, has long been hypothsized to be deeply bound objects based on phenomenological as well as first principles lattice QCD investigations. However, predictions on the fate of a Tbc tetraquark, composed of a bottom and a charm quarks, a up and a down antiquark, remain scattered.

[Image Credits]: Dr. Archana Radhakrishnan

In a recent publication in Physical Review Letters, Padmanath Madanagopalan from The Institute of Mathematical Sciences Chennai (IMSc Chennai), along with his collaborators Nilmani Mathur and Archana Radhakrishnan from Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR Mumbai) report a first-principles calculation, wherein they predicts the existence of a novel Tbc tetraquark. To this end, they simulated the numerical scattering experiments involving bottom mesons B* and charmed mesons D on state-of-the-art lattice QCD ensembles generated using Markov chain Monte Carlo methods. Their results suggest strong evidence for attractive interaction between the B* and D mesons, pointing to the possibility of a bound Tbc tetraquark (See figure above for a schematic picture). Working with advanced computational and rigorous analysis procedures, the authors determine the binding energy of such a state to be ~43 MeV with unprecedented control over the systematic uncertainties. With the heightened accessibility of the required energy range and luminosity for production and detection at present experimental facilities, there's a realistic prospect that this newly projected particle, or a related iteration, could be unveiled within the next 5 to 10 years. The significant challenge lies in detecting the predicted deep binding experimentally. Nevertheless, this deep binding, along with its variation with changing pion mass, presents intriguing features from a theoretical standpoint. It serves as a catalyst for further theoretical explorations into heavier exotic subatomic particles, with anticipation towards their potential discovery in future-generation experiments.

First-principles investigations of particle interactions such as this require a detailed knowledge of quantum field theory and large-scale High-Performance Computing (HPC) facility at disposal. Lattice gauge theorists such as M. Padmanath and Nilmani Mathur utilize some of the largest HPC facilities across the globe, such as those in Germany (GCS/NIC Julich), the US (NESRC), India (ILGTI) to investigate the different facets of nuclear interactions that operate at the scale of femto-Universe (0.000000000000001 meters). In this recently published work, Padmanath and Nilmani Mathur have utilized the computational facilities with the Indian Lattice Gauge Theory Initiative (ILGTI), TIFR Mumbai, and at IMSc Chennai to carry out the investigations. The large interaction strength of the strong force at this scale prohibits any acceptable analytic solutions using first principles from fundamental theory limiting one to resort to numerical procedures with huge HPC requirements. Working with such facilities, the authors look forward to performing several other cutting-edge calculations that can shed light on hadron-hadron interactions, eventually leading to a deeper quark-level understanding of the hadron exotics.
